The problem

A trading call is easy to fake after the fact.

  • Signal groups delete the calls that lost. Screenshots get edited.
  • A bot that grades itself is marking its own exam.

How it works

Publish, seal, price, grade.

1PublishSMT World posts the committee's call on 8 coins every hour: LONG, SHORT or WAIT, a conviction and the reason.
2SealA keeper writes the whole snapshot to Monad, plus the hash of every reason. Old, repeated or partial snapshots are refused.
3PriceRedStone signs a price every 10 s. Entry is the first tick after the seal block; exit is 4 h later.
4GradeAnyone submits the two signed prices. The contract checks 3 of 5 signatures, subtracts the 12 bp fee and keeps score.

Trust model

What can't be faked.

HindsightThe entry price is signed after the call is on-chain.
The priceThe contract recovers RedStone's signatures at the exact second. It never takes a number from me.
Cherry-pickingEvery coin is sealed every time. A call nobody grades within 22 h counts as a miss that paid its fee.
EditsNo owner, no admin key, no upgrade path. Deployed through a CREATE2 proxy, so the code fixes the address.

Why Monad

Cheap enough to seal every call.

  • 8 calls per seal, 24 seals a day, plus grades that each carry a dozen signed prices. About 0.015 testnet MON a seal.
  • The seal lands seconds after the keeper reads the feed, and the entry tick follows within 10 seconds.
  • If every receipt cost real money, you'd only seal the calls you liked. That is the problem again.
